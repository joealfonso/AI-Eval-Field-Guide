Templates for AI evaluation

Three plain-text templates you can copy into a document, a ticket, or a repository. Each is on this page and also downloadable as a Markdown file.

They are licensed CC BY 4.0: copy, adapt, and share them with credit to the AI Evaluation Field Guide. They are starting points, not a standard, and filling one in does not by itself show that an evaluation is sound. Delete what does not fit and add what does.

AI vendor questionnaire

Thirty-four questions in four parts (does it work, contract and operations, data handling, security and misuse) to send a vendor or internal team before you rely on an AI system. Each has what a good answer and a red flag look like, with a 0 to 2 scoring scheme, a list of which questions to ask at which risk tier, and a summary sheet. Part A comes from the pattern pages; parts B to D are this guide’s own.

Send this to a vendor or internal team before you rely on an AI system. Ask for answers in writing, with the evidence attached. It has four parts: A evidence that the system works, B contract and operations, C data handling, and D security and misuse. Each question has a “good answer” and a “red flag”, which describe what a specific, checkable reply looks like. Neither column is a pass or fail on its own: a weak answer can be fixed by evidence you have not seen yet.

Parts B, C, and D are this guide’s own questions. Part A comes from the patterns at https://evalfieldguide.com/patterns/ (for example, /patterns/data-contamination). Security and contract practice are not covered by the research behind the patterns, so those parts name no pattern and make no claim about what vendors usually do. Have your security, privacy, and legal teams review them; they are not legal or compliance advice.

How to score

Score each question you are required to ask (see “Which questions at which tier” below) with one of three marks.

  • 0: no answer, a refusal, or a reply with no evidence behind it.
  • 1: a partial answer, or an answer without evidence you can check.
  • 2: a specific answer with evidence you have seen or can check yourself.
  • N/A: the question does not apply to this system. Write down why.

The score describes how well the vendor evidenced the answer, not how good the system is. It is one reviewer’s judgment and this scheme has not been validated, so have a second reviewer score independently and discuss any difference of more than one point (raters disagree, see The Gold Standard Myth).

Add up points for each part separately, and divide by two times the number of questions you scored, which is the most that part could give. Do not combine the parts into one overall number. An average can hide a failure (The Averaging Trap), and a vendor strong on A but silent on C should not look fine. Instead report each part’s points, the lowest part, and every required question that scored 0.

Which questions at which tier

The tiers and the choice of questions are this guide’s own model, not a standard. Ask everything for a higher tier too.

TierAsk these

A required question that scores 0 is a stop until you have a way to cover it yourself, such as your own trial, a contract term, or a technical control. Write that cover next to the score.

Part A: Does the system work?

#QuestionGood answerRed flagPattern
A1What evidence shows the system works on data like ours, gathered by someone other than the vendor?Names the data, who collected it, and when; offers a trial on our own dataOnly vendor-run results, or “customers are happy”The Functionality Fallacy
A2What does the headline benchmark measure, and how close is it to our task?Describes the task and format, and says what a high score would not tell usTreats the benchmark name as the capability (“reasoning”, “understanding”)The Construct Gap
A3Was the test set ever used for tuning, prompt iteration, or choosing between versions?A locked test set kept apart from the set used for iteration, with a count of how often it was consultedNo separate test set, or “we check it after every change”Adaptive Overfitting
A4What overlap or contamination checks were run between the test data and the training data? Were results confirmed on data created after the training cutoff?Describes the method and the result, or says plainly that no check was run“The data is proprietary” with no check describedData Contamination
A5How many prompt variants, runs, and settings were tried before this result was reported?A number, the range across variants, and the same variants used for competitorsOne prompt, one run, or no answerPrompt Sensitivity; Once Is Not Reliable
A6What is the 95% confidence interval on each score, and how many items and runs does it rest on?Intervals or standard errors on every score, and an interval on the gap between systems (paired, if they were scored on the same items)Single numbers with no sample size; a “win” of a point or two with no interval on the gapNo Error Bars, No Result
A7What is the pass rate when every run must succeed, not the best run?Reports average and all-runs-pass rates, with the number of runs per itemReports pass@k or best-of-k onlyOnce Is Not Reliable
A8Which user groups, languages, or input types matter here, and what are the results for each? What is the worst-performing slice?A breakdown, the worst slice named, and enough items in each slice to judge itOne overall averageThe Averaging Trap
A9What baselines were included: the simplest thing that could work, and our current process? Did they get the same tuning effort?Simple and current-process baselines with equal tuning budgetsCompared only with weaker or untuned alternativesThe Baseline Rule
A10If an AI model graded the answers, was it checked against human judgments on a sample? Is the grader from the same model family?Reports human-grader agreement on a sample, controls for order and length“We used GPT-4 to score it” with no validationJudge Bias
A11What does each task cost in tokens or dollars, and what is the latency, next to every accuracy number?Cost and latency beside accuracy, compared with a cheaper baseline at a matched budgetAccuracy onlyThe Cost Frontier
A12Which common benchmarks are missing from your results table, and why?Says why each was left out; the suite was chosen before results were seenOnly the benchmarks the system winsThe Benchmark Lottery
A13Can we rerun the evaluation? Are the model version, prompts, settings, data version, and scoring code recorded, and are raw outputs saved?Yes, with a version or date, and a plan for rerunning when the model is updated“The model changes continuously” with no versioningThe Reproducibility Rule
A14When was the evaluation data collected, from which users, and how does it compare with our inputs?Dates and sources, and a plan to validate on our data before rolloutUnknown provenance, or data from a different populationDistribution Shift
A15What changed for real users in a pilot, measured how? Which lab results did not hold up?Names pilot sites, measures, and at least one result that did not carry overNo pilot, or only satisfaction quotesThe Lab-to-Field Gap
A16What does the system do when it is wrong, and how often does that happen? How will we find out?Describes failure types, rates, and a monitoring and escalation route“It rarely fails”The Functionality Fallacy; Presence, Not Absence
A17How hard did anyone try to make the system fail: which prompts, tools, attempts, and who did the red-teaming?Describes the effort and the testers’ expertise; negative results phrased as “under these conditions we did not observe X”“We found no problems” with no description of the effortPresence, Not Absence

If a vendor will not or cannot answer, you can often run the check yourself. For A3 to A8, run a small pilot on your own locked test set, use the sample-size page to choose the number of items, and score every system on the same items with a paired comparison. For A12, choose the benchmark suite yourself before looking at any results. For A13, record the version and date and rerun your pilot whenever the vendor changes the model.

Part B: Contract and operations

#QuestionGood answerRed flagPattern
B1Can we pin a specific model version, and what is the notice before it changes?Named versions or dated snapshots, written notice of changes before they take effectThe model changes under the same name with no noticeThe Reproducibility Rule
B2How long is a version supported before it is retired, and what happens to our results and prompts when it is?A stated support window, a migration period, and a way to rerun our evaluation on the replacementRetirement with short or no noticeThe Reproducibility Rule
B3May we run our own evaluation, including a pilot on our own data, before and after signing, and keep the results?Yes, with test access that matches production and no restriction on sharing results internallyTerms that bar benchmarking or require approval to share resultsDistribution Shift
B4Can the contract include an acceptance test on our data, with the metrics, threshold, and sample size fixed in advance?Agrees to a test we design, with a remedy if it is not metAccepts only the vendor’s own reported figuresThe Functionality Fallacy
B5How do you tell us about incidents, such as a serious failure, a data exposure, or a model regression, and how fast?A written process, a named contact, and a stated notification timeNo process, or notice only on requestThis guide’s own question
B6Is there independent assurance we can see, such as a third-party assessment or audit, and may we or our assessor test the system?A recent report with its scope stated, and agreement to a scoped testOnly a marketing summary, or no outside reviewThis guide’s own question
B7What happens when we leave: can we export our data, and what is deleted and confirmed in writing?Export format, deletion steps, a deletion deadline, and written confirmationDeletion is unspecified or by request onlyThis guide’s own question

Part C: Data handling

#QuestionGood answerRed flagPattern
C1Are our inputs and outputs stored, for how long, and where?A stated retention period and location, with a way to turn retention offRetention is unknown or open-endedThis guide’s own question
C2Are our inputs or outputs used to train or tune any model, yours or anyone else’s?No by default and in writing, or an explicit opt-in we controlUsed for training unless we find and change a settingData Contamination
C3Who else processes our data, such as subprocessors or other model providers, and in which countries?A current list, with notice of changesNo list, or “partners” with no namesThis guide’s own question
C4Can we have specific data deleted on request, and how do you confirm it?A described process, a deadline, and written confirmationNo process, or deletion that excludes backups and logs with no explanationThis guide’s own question
C5Who at the vendor can see our data, and is access logged and reviewable?Role-based access, access logs, and a way to review themBroad access with no loggingThis guide’s own question

Part D: Security and misuse

Use these when the system reads untrusted text (web pages, email, documents, user messages) or can call tools, query your data, or take actions. If it does neither, mark the question N/A and say so.

#QuestionGood answerRed flagPattern
D1How has the system been tested against instructions hidden in the content it reads, and what were the results?Describes the test method and attempts, reports what got through, and says what was done about it“The model is trained to refuse” with no test describedThis guide’s own question
D2What can the system do on its own, with which permissions, and which actions need a person to confirm first?Least-privilege permissions, a list of actions, human confirmation for anything hard to undoBroad access by default, or actions taken with no confirmationThis guide’s own question
D3How could data from our systems leave through the AI, for example through tool calls, links, or generated output, and what stops that?Names the routes and the controls, and offers to test themNo analysis, or “that cannot happen”This guide’s own question
D4How hard did anyone try to misuse or break the system, who did it, and what did they find?Describes the effort, the testers’ expertise, and findings; negative results phrased as “under these conditions we did not observe X”“We found no problems” with no description of the effortPresence, Not Absence
D5How do you receive and fix vulnerability reports, and can you show recent fixes?A public reporting route, a stated response time, and a record of past fixesNo route, or no recordThis guide’s own question

  • Reported as: average, range across variants, and all-runs-pass rate.
