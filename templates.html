Templates for AI evaluation

Three plain-text templates you can copy into a document, a ticket, or a repository. Each is on this page and also downloadable as a Markdown file.

They are licensed CC BY 4.0: copy, adapt, and share them with credit to the AI Evaluation Field Guide. They are starting points, not a standard, and filling one in does not by itself show that an evaluation is sound. Delete what does not fit and add what does.

AI vendor questionnaire

Seventeen questions to send a vendor or internal team before you rely on an AI system, each with what a good answer and a red flag look like. Built from the questions on the pattern pages and the buying-a-model list.

Download (Markdown)

View on this page

Send this to a vendor or internal team before you rely on an AI system. Ask for answers in writing, with the evidence attached. “Good answer” describes what a specific, checkable reply looks like. “Red flag” describes replies that should lead to a follow-up. Neither column is a pass or fail on its own: a weak answer can be fixed by evidence you have not seen yet.

Pattern names refer to pages on https://evalfieldguide.com/patterns/ (for example, /patterns/data-contamination).

1What evidence shows the system works on data like ours, gathered by someone other than the vendor?Names the data, who collected it, and when; offers a trial on our own dataOnly vendor-run results, or “customers are happy”The Functionality Fallacy
2What does the headline benchmark measure, and how close is it to our task?Describes the task and format, and says what a high score would not tell usTreats the benchmark name as the capability (“reasoning”, “understanding”)The Construct Gap
3Was the test set ever used for tuning, prompt iteration, or choosing between versions?A locked test set kept apart from the set used for iteration, with a count of how often it was consultedNo separate test set, or “we check it after every change”Adaptive Overfitting
4What overlap or contamination checks were run between the test data and the training data? Were results confirmed on data created after the training cutoff?Describes the method and the result, or says plainly that no check was run“The data is proprietary” with no check describedData Contamination
5How many prompt variants, runs, and settings were tried before this result was reported?A number, the range across variants, and the same variants used for competitorsOne prompt, one run, or no answerPrompt Sensitivity; Once Is Not Reliable
6What is the 95% confidence interval on each score, and how many items and runs does it rest on?Intervals or standard errors on every score; gaps compared on the same itemsSingle numbers with no sample size; a “win” of a point or twoNo Error Bars, No Result
7What is the pass rate when every run must succeed, not the best run?Reports average and all-runs-pass rates, with the number of runs per itemReports pass@k or best-of-k onlyOnce Is Not Reliable
8Which user groups, languages, or input types matter here, and what are the results for each? What is the worst-performing slice?A breakdown, the worst slice named, and enough items in each slice to judge itOne overall averageThe Averaging Trap
9What baselines were included: the simplest thing that could work, and our current process? Did they get the same tuning effort?Simple and current-process baselines with equal tuning budgetsCompared only with weaker or untuned alternativesThe Baseline Rule
10If an AI model graded the answers, was it checked against human judgments on a sample? Is the grader from the same model family?Reports human-grader agreement on a sample, controls for order and length“We used GPT-4 to score it” with no validationJudge Bias
11What does each task cost in tokens or dollars, and what is the latency, next to every accuracy number?Cost and latency beside accuracy, compared with a cheaper baseline at a matched budgetAccuracy onlyThe Cost Frontier
12Which common benchmarks are missing from your results table, and why?Says why each was left out; the suite was chosen before results were seenOnly the benchmarks the system winsThe Benchmark Lottery
13Can we rerun the evaluation? Are the model version, prompts, settings, data version, and scoring code recorded, and are raw outputs saved?Yes, with a version or date, and a plan for rerunning when the model is updated“The model changes continuously” with no versioningThe Reproducibility Rule
14When was the evaluation data collected, from which users, and how does it compare with our inputs?Dates and sources, and a plan to validate on our data before rolloutUnknown provenance, or data from a different populationDistribution Shift
15What changed for real users in a pilot, measured how? Which lab results did not hold up?Names pilot sites, measures, and at least one result that did not carry overNo pilot, or only satisfaction quotesThe Lab-to-Field Gap
16What does the system do when it is wrong, and how often does that happen? How will we find out?Describes failure types, rates, and a monitoring and escalation route“It rarely fails”The Functionality Fallacy; Presence, Not Absence
17How hard did anyone try to make the system fail: which prompts, tools, attempts, and who did the red-teaming?Describes the effort and the testers’ expertise; negative results phrased as “under these conditions we did not observe X”“We found no problems” with no description of the effortPresence, Not Absence

Follow-up

  • Which answers came with evidence attached?
  • Which answers were “we do not know”? An honest “we have not tested that” is more useful than a confident reply with nothing behind it.
  • What can we check ourselves in a small trial before signing?

Eval plan one-pager

Fill this in before you run an evaluation: the decision, what is measured, data, locked test set, metrics, baselines, repeats, grader validation, stopping rules, and the owner.

Fill this in before running the evaluation and keep it with the results. Writing the choices down first helps make them credible later: metrics, test set, and stopping rules chosen after seeing results can be bent to fit them.

1. The decision

What will be decided with this evaluation, by whom, and by when? What result would change the decision?

Example: “Whether to roll the support assistant out to all agents by 30 June. We roll out if it resolves at least as many tickets as the current process without more escalations.”

2. What we are measuring

Summary

One or two sentences: what was decided or recommended, and how confident we are.
