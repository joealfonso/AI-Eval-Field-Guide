AI Readiness Review

A more rigorous instrument for launch review. Five screening questions come first, then 27 criteria across seven dimensions. Under each criterion, the labels marked "Based on" name the standards and guidelines it draws on; the key at the bottom of the page explains each one. Some criteria are marked as gates.

Scope. As of 19 October 2026. This is a design and launch-review checklist, not legal advice, a conformity assessment, or a compliance determination. The standards named under “Based on” are sources the criteria draw on; this page does not claim that meeting a criterion satisfies any of them. EU AI Act Articles 13 and 14 set requirements for high-risk AI systems only; Article 50 covers certain other systems, including ones that interact directly with people. As amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (in force 27 July 2026), the Commission’s AI Act Service Desk lists the high-risk rules as applying from 2 December 2027 (Annex III systems) and 2 August 2028 (AI embedded in products covered by Annex I), and Article 50 from 2 August 2026. Check current application dates before relying on them.

1Screening — five questions asked before scoring

  1. S1Does the output influence a decision about a person’s employment, finances, health, housing, legal status, education, or benefits?
  2. S2Can the system take an action, or commit a result, without a human confirming it first?
  3. S3Does the feature process personal, sensitive, or identifiable data?
  4. S4Will non-expert, vulnerable, or unsupported users rely on the output without a specialist intermediary?
  5. S5Are the effects of a wrong output slow, costly, or impossible to reverse?

D1Capability disclosure

  1. D1.1Users are told they are interacting with an AI system, clearly and at or before first interaction.Gate · Tier 2Based on
    • AI Act Art. 50

  2. D1.2Expected reliability is communicated in terms the user can act on, not as an unqualified capability claim.Based on
    • HAX G1–G18

  4. D1.4Expectation-setting is staged across the experience rather than front-loaded into a single disclaimer.Based on

D3Human agency and oversight

  1. D3.1The user can see what the system proposes to do before it takes effect.Gate · Tier 2Based on
    • AI Act Art. 14
    • HAX G1–G18
  2. D3.2A person can disregard, override, or reverse the output without engineering assistance.Gate · Tier 2Based on
    • AI Act Art. 14
    • HAX G1–G18
  3. D3.3Consequential actions require explicit human confirmation; the system does not execute them silently.Gate · Tier 2NoteA design practice rather than a requirement of a listed source. It is related to the human-oversight duties in AI Act Art. 14 for high-risk systems, which call for oversight tools and the ability to disregard, override, reverse, or stop the system (see D3.2), not for confirmation of every action.

KeySources — what each “Based on” label refers to

  • AI Act Art. 50 EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), Article 50: transparency obligations for providers and deployers of certain AI systems, including telling people when they are interacting directly with an AI system unless that is obvious. It is not limited to high-risk systems.
  • HAX G1–G18 Microsoft’s Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction (Amershi et al., CHI 2019): eighteen guidelines for how an AI feature should behave with the people using it.
  • AI Act Art. 13 EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), Article 13: transparency and provision of information to deployers. It sets a requirement for high-risk AI systems, about the instructions for use given to the organizations that deploy them. Criteria that cite it apply the same idea to what users are shown.
  • NIST AI 100-1 NIST’s Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0, NIST AI 100-1, January 2023), a U.S. voluntary framework for identifying and managing AI risk. NIST has said that AI RMF 1.0 is being revised.
  • PAIR Guidebook Google’s People + AI Guidebook, design guidance for AI-powered products.
  • NIST AI 600-1 NIST AI 600-1, Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile (July 2024), which applies the AI RMF to generative AI.
  • ISO/IEC 42001 ISO/IEC 42001:2023, Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Management system (edition 1, December 2023): requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and improving an AI management system in an organization. It works at organization level; criteria that cite it draw on its management practices.
  • WCAG 2.2 W3C’s Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2, a W3C Recommendation (12 December 2024).
  • W3C AI accessibility draft “Accessibility of machine learning and generative AI”, an Editor’s Draft (28 March 2026 when checked) from the W3C Accessible Platform Architectures Working Group. It is a work in progress, not a standard, and is not endorsed by W3C or its members. Background reading, not a requirement.
  • AI Act Art. 14 EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), Article 14: human oversight. It sets a requirement for high-risk AI systems: suitable tools so that people can oversee the system, and the ability to disregard, override, reverse, or stop it. It does not require confirmation of every action.
  • Parasuraman 1997 Parasuraman and Riley (1997), “Humans and automation: use, misuse, disuse, abuse”, Human Factors: research on how people use, over-rely on, and neglect automation.
  • Mosier 1996 Mosier and Skitka (1996), “Human decision makers and automated decision aids: made for each other?”, in Automation and Human Performance: research on automation bias in human decision-making.

This page lists the instrument (AIRR v1.0). This page does not score a system or assign a tier; use it as a checklist. Tiers: Tier 1 to Tier 3 are this instrument’s own risk tiers, running from lower to higher risk. They are not EU AI Act risk categories and are unrelated to the check labels on the Design Rubric. A Gate badge marks a criterion treated as a gate: it should be met before launch for a feature at that tier or higher. “Gate · Tier 2” applies at Tier 2 and Tier 3; “Gate · Tier 3” applies at Tier 3 only. Criteria with no badge are not gates. The page does not say how to place a feature in a tier; the five screening questions are the starting point for that judgment.
