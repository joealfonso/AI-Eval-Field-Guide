AI Readiness Review

A more rigorous instrument for launch review. Five screening questions come first, then 27 criteria across seven dimensions. Each criterion cites the standards behind it, and some are marked as gates.

1Screening — five questions asked before scoring

  1. S1Does the output influence a decision about a person’s employment, finances, health, housing, legal status, education, or benefits?
  2. S2Can the system take an action, or commit a result, without a human confirming it first?
  3. S3Does the feature process personal, sensitive, or identifiable data?
  4. S4Will non-expert, vulnerable, or unsupported users rely on the output without a specialist intermediary?
  5. S5Are the effects of a wrong output slow, costly, or impossible to reverse?

D1Capability disclosure

  1. D1.1Users are told they are interacting with an AI system, clearly and at or before first interaction.Gate · T2
    • AI Act Art. 50
    • HAX G1–G18
  2. D1.2Expected reliability is communicated in terms the user can act on, not as an unqualified capability claim.
    • HAX G1–G18
    • AI Act Art. 13
    • NIST AI 100-1
  3. D1.3Scope boundaries are stated: the interface communicates what the system is not for.
    • HAX G1–G18
    • PAIR Guidebook
    • AI Act Art. 13
  4. D1.4Expectation-setting is staged across the experience rather than front-loaded into a single disclaimer.
    • PAIR Guidebook

D2Output legibility

  1. D2.1Uncertainty is expressed per output and reflects actual model confidence rather than a fixed decorative label.
    • NIST AI 100-1
    • HAX G1–G18
    • NIST AI 600-1
  2. D2.2The reason for a given output is available at the point of decision, in plain language.Gate · T2
    • HAX G1–G18
    • AI Act Art. 13
    • NIST AI 100-1
  3. D2.3Output is traceable to the inputs, records, or rules that produced it.
    • NIST AI 100-1
    • ISO/IEC 42001
    • AI Act Art. 13
  4. D2.4Confidence signals and explanations are conveyed non-visually as well as visually.Gate · T2
    • WCAG 2.2
    • AI Act Art. 50
    • W3C AI-A11y

D3Human agency and oversight

  1. D3.1The user can see what the system proposes to do before it takes effect.Gate · T2
    • AI Act Art. 14
    • HAX G1–G18
  2. D3.2A person can disregard, override, or reverse the output without engineering assistance.Gate · T2
    • AI Act Art. 14
    • HAX G1–G18
  3. D3.3Consequential actions require explicit human confirmation; the system does not execute them silently.Gate · T2
    • AI Act Art. 14
  4. D3.4The design actively counters automation bias rather than encouraging uncritical acceptance.Gate · T3
    • AI Act Art. 14
    • Parasuraman 1997
    • Mosier 1996
    • NIST AI 600-1

D4Uncertainty and failure design

  1. D4.1An explicit “cannot determine” state exists and is structurally distinct from a confident answer.Gate · T2
    • PAIR Guidebook
    • HAX G1–G18
    • NIST AI 600-1
  2. D4.2Low-confidence output receives different visual and interaction treatment from high-confidence output.
    • HAX G1–G18
    • PAIR Guidebook
  3. D4.3A designed recovery path exists for AI-specific errors, separate from generic system error handling.Gate · T2
    • PAIR Guidebook
    • HAX G1–G18
  4. D4.4Failure is contained: one bad output does not block, corrupt, or silently alter unrelated work.
    • NIST AI 100-1
    • PAIR Guidebook

D5Equity and accessibility

  1. D5.1The AI-specific interface has been tested with assistive technology, not only reviewed visually or inherited from a component library.Gate · T2
    • WCAG 2.2
    • W3C AI-A11y
  2. D5.2Performance disparities across user subgroups, languages, or regions have been measured rather than assumed absent.Gate · T3
    • NIST AI 100-1
    • NIST AI 600-1
    • AI Act Art. 13
    • HAX G1–G18
  3. D5.3AI-generated accessibility content is human-reviewed before it is relied upon.
    • W3C AI-A11y
    • WCAG 2.2
  4. D5.4The experience adapts to user context and stakes rather than offering one undifferentiated path.
    • HAX G1–G18
    • PAIR Guidebook

D6Demonstrated value

  1. D6.1The efficiency or quality claim is measured against a real pre-AI baseline, not estimated.
    • NIST AI 100-1
    • ISO/IEC 42001
  2. D6.2Complexity is genuinely removed rather than displaced into a downstream step, another team, or a later moment.
    • NIST AI 100-1
    • PAIR Guidebook
  3. D6.3A granular feedback mechanism exists and demonstrably routes into system improvement.
    • HAX G1–G18
    • PAIR Guidebook
  4. D6.4Post-deployment monitoring is defined, with a named owner and a threshold that triggers action.
    • NIST AI 100-1
    • ISO/IEC 42001

D7Accountability record

  1. D7.1Known limitations are documented in a form that actually reaches the people who deploy and use the system.Gate · T2
    • AI Act Art. 13
    • ISO/IEC 42001
  2. D7.2A named human owner is accountable for this feature’s behavior in production.
    • NIST AI 100-1
    • ISO/IEC 42001
  3. D7.3An impact assessment covering affected individuals and groups was completed before launch.Gate · T3
    • ISO/IEC 42001
    • NIST AI 100-1
